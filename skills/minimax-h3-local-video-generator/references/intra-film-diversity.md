# Intra-film layout and motion diversity

Use this reference for every project. Preserve style coherence without confusing coherence with repetition. A strong film keeps one design grammar while expressing different visual sentences.

## 1. Separate invariants from variables

Lock the style invariants that make the film feel like one world:

- palette hierarchy and contrast logic;
- medium, material, texture, edge, outline, shadow, and surface response;
- typography family and hierarchy principles;
- motif DNA and icon vocabulary;
- easing character, acceleration profile, and directional tendency;
- character identity, product geometry, or other continuity anchors.

Vary the structural axes that make each beat feel newly designed:

- layout skeleton and occupied regions;
- subject scale, crop, angle, and position;
- focal count and hierarchy;
- negative-space topology;
- background, middle-ground, subject, foreground, and edge-layer topology;
- typography-image relationship;
- panel, mask, crop, overlap, repetition, or spatial-division logic;
- primary graphic operation;
- primary motion carrier and transformation mechanism;
- camera behavior and transition carrier.

## 2. Apply the distinct-composition test

Count a major landing as distinct only when at least three structural axes change from every earlier major landing. A new character crop, word, color accent, card image, prop, or small coordinate adjustment does not make a new composition when the same spatial massing and hierarchy remain.

Use these rejection tests:

- **Content-swap test**: if replacing the subject and words leaves the same poster template, the layout is repeated.
- **Silhouette test**: reduce the frame to large light and dark masses; if the silhouette is nearly identical, require stronger structural change.
- **Typography-removal test**: if the remaining panels and subject occupy the same regions, changing the headline alone does not count.
- **Motion-removal test**: if two shots settle into the same spatial skeleton after motion stops, different transition effects do not make them distinct.

Allow one exact layout skeleton to appear only once by default. A deliberate callback must be labeled `A-prime`, serve a new narrative or rhythmic purpose, and change at least three axes such as scale, crop, focal count, negative-space shape, layer topology, type-image relationship, or camera orientation. Exempt only the approved final readable hold.

## 3. Build the layout-diversity matrix

Before writing the H3 prompt, create one row per major beat:

| Beat | Layout ID | Spatial skeleton | Subject scale/crop | Focal count | Negative space | Layer topology | Type-image relationship | Primary graphic operation | Motion carrier | Exit mechanism | Distinctness proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Assign the shot-change and distinct-layout budgets together:

- restrained or ceremonial references: roughly four to six distinct compositions;
- medium-energy character, product, or editorial references: roughly six to eight;
- dense, collage, playful, action-led, or high-energy references: roughly seven to ten, including a justified acceleration burst;
- excluding the final hold, at least 70% of major landings should have independent spatial skeletons unless serial repetition is explicit reference evidence.

Do not increase the score by inserting redundant cuts. A cut counts only when it introduces new information and a structurally different landing or a necessary moving transition between two different landings.

## 4. Rotate motion mechanisms inside one style family

Build coherence from shared material response, easing, directional logic, timing character, typography family, and motif DNA. Build diversity by rotating the active carrier and spatial operation.

For dense, editorial, collage, playful, or action-led work, normally use at least four observable mechanisms supported by the reference. Possible mechanism categories include panel reflow, mask or crop reveal, typography construction, shape transformation, subject-led match action, foreground occlusion, parallax relay, camera-scale handoff, material tear or fold, trace or draw, smear or echo, light or scan travel, and spatial collapse or expansion. Treat these as categories, not presets.

Do not repeat the same complete chain of `entry → assembly → settle → exit` in consecutive shots or across several hero beats. Rotate which layer leads:

- character or product action;
- typography;
- panel or collage structure;
- foreground occlusion;
- background geometry;
- camera movement;
- light, line, material, or particle response.

Keep cut-to-cut momentum. A new mechanism must inherit, redirect, transform, or absorb the previous shot's energy rather than creating a rest-to-rest reset.

## 5. Translate diversity into the prompt

State global style invariants once near the beginning. Repeating the same global paragraph and motion verbs inside every shot encourages the model to reuse one template.

For each shot, explicitly name:

1. its layout ID and new spatial scaffold;
2. the three or more axes that distinguish it from earlier landings;
3. subject scale, crop, focal hierarchy, and layer topology;
4. the typography-image relationship;
5. one primary graphic operation and one primary motion carrier;
6. entry path, easing, landing, internal microbeat, exit transformation, and momentum handoff;
7. any `A-prime` callback and how it has been structurally transformed.

Avoid shot descriptions that differ only through adjectives, subject details, copy, or minor coordinates. Use different observable construction verbs and spatial consequences while retaining the approved style grammar.

## 6. Audit generated results

Create a contact sheet at roughly 0.5-second intervals and add denser samples around transitions. Label every major landing with its layout ID.

Reject or target-rerun when:

- the same spatial skeleton appears more than once without an approved `A-prime` transformation;
- fewer than 70% of non-final major landings are structurally independent without reference evidence for repetition;
- multiple shots amount to the same poster with different subject crops, text, cards, or accents;
- adjacent shots reuse the same motion carrier and entry-assembly-settle-exit chain;
- a dense or editorial film uses fewer than four observable reference-derived motion mechanisms;
- a non-final composition survives longer than about 1.5 seconds with only drift, breathing, scale creep, or text completion;
- adding more cuts has increased activity but not layout or motion-mechanism diversity.

Correct repeated beats by redesigning their spatial skeleton, type-image relationship, layer topology, focal scale, and motion carrier. Do not hide repetition with filler decoration, random effects, extra sound effects, or arbitrary camera shake.
