# Cross-modal style coherence

Treat the approved style reference as the artistic source of truth for the complete film. Its transferable design logic must govern rendering, composition, density, motion, typography, camera, transitions, edit rhythm, BGM, and supporting sound design. Do not choose any of these domains independently because a treatment is fashionable or worked in a previous project.

Style coherence is not layout repetition. Lock palette, material, typography family, motif DNA, edge treatment, easing character, and directional logic, then use `intra-film-diversity.md` to vary spatial skeletons, focal scale, negative-space topology, layer structure, typography-image relationships, graphic operations, motion carriers, and transition mechanisms across the film.

Exception: when the user uploads a BGM and explicitly approves it as the complete artistic style guide, the audio contract becomes the primary cross-modal source of truth. Style images become secondary visual evidence and may contribute only compatible rendering details. When uploaded BGM is timing-only, this exception does not apply and the selected visual reference cluster remains the artistic source of truth.

## 1. Resolve multiple references before designing

Analyze every supplied style image or video separately. Label each asset and record its medium, era or cultural cues, geometry, material, density, composition, typography, energy, and implied rhythm.

- Group references only when their design logic is compatible.
- Identify one primary style direction from the user's wording, asset emphasis, or stated preference.
- Use secondary references only for explicitly assigned aspects that do not contradict the primary direction.
- If references form incompatible clusters and the intended primary direction is unclear, present the clusters as distinct directions for approval before writing the production prompt.
- Do not average incompatible references into a generic hybrid. For requested variants, one coherent reference cluster may define each variant when the user approves a style comparison.

Character references remain the source of identity and palette when present. Re-map the primary style direction through the character palette without replacing the style reference's material, layout, typography, motion, rhythm, or music logic.

## 2. Build the style contract

Summarize the selected reference direction as a compact contract:

- medium, finish, and surface behavior;
- era, cultural, genre, and production cues that are visibly supported;
- palette hierarchy, contrast, brightness, saturation, and temperature;
- geometry, motif family, edge language, and silhouette character;
- composition, grid, whitespace, overlap, cropping, and depth;
- visual density and detail hierarchy;
- typography personality, construction, hierarchy, and spatial role;
- implied weight, elasticity, precision, aggression, softness, playfulness, restraint, or ceremony;
- dominant direction, motion material, timing contour, and transition logic;
- explicit prohibitions: visual, typographic, motion, musical, and editing choices that would break the reference tone.

## 3. Complete the coherence matrix

Before the creative proposal, fill every row:

| Domain | Evidence from reference | Derived decision | Prohibited mismatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rendering and material | medium, surface, texture, edge behavior | finish, layer treatment, lighting, deformation | unrelated rendering language |
| Composition and density | grid, whitespace, overlap, crop, hierarchy | shot layouts, occupied regions, layer stack | generic sparse or maximal layout |
| Motion | geometry, material, weight, directional force | verbs, easing, amplitude, arcs or rails, overlap | reusable motion preset |
| Typography | visible type or inferred graphic grammar | family category, weight, case, tracking, outline, shadow, grid role | unrelated fashionable type style |
| Typography motion | type construction and material | draw, stamp, crop, tile, reflow, collide, scan, stretch, or another evidenced behavior | generic fade, scale, or slide system |
| Camera and transitions | depth, focal hierarchy, spatial tendency, motifs | camera path, amplitude, transition carriers | unrelated cinematic spectacle |
| Edit rhythm | density, repetition, visual weight, implied energy | shot budget, interval contour, bursts, breath, climax | reused timestamp skeleton |
| BGM | era, material, density, geometry, energy, typographic personality | genre family, timbre, instruments, BPM, groove, phrase shape, dynamics | arbitrary trending track or conflicting production style |
| Sound effects | material and motion events | small coherent effect palette and mix priority | effects from an unrelated material world |

Do not approve a blank or purely adjectival row. Each decision must cite observable evidence or a clearly marked inference from combined evidence.

## 4. Derive typography from the reference

When typography is visible, analyze its category, width, weight, case, tracking, line spacing, alignment, outline, shadow, fill, repetition, cropping, distortion, and relation to imagery. Transfer those rules without copying unauthorized words or logos.

When no typography is visible, infer it from the reference's geometry, material, composition, era, density, and tone. Keep the inference conservative. A quiet photographic or luxury reference does not justify playful bubble lettering; a dense pop collage does not justify a sparse corporate sans-serif lockup unless the user requests contrast.

Derive static type design and type motion together. The same material logic should govern both: print-derived type may stamp, overprint, misregister, tear, or crop; measured industrial type may scan, align, calibrate, and lock; soft organic type may stretch or recombine; restrained editorial type may reflow, mask, and reorder precisely. These are examples, not presets.

## 5. Derive BGM from visual evidence

Translate the selected visual direction into one coherent music direction by considering evidence jointly:

- era and cultural cues constrain the production family and instrument vocabulary;
- material and texture suggest timbre, articulation, acoustic or electronic character, and surface treatment;
- visual density suggests arrangement density, number of simultaneous musical layers, and rhythmic detail;
- geometry and edge character suggest pulse shape, attack, sustain, syncopation, and precision;
- composition and whitespace suggest phrase spacing, rests, sustain, and dynamic breathing room;
- contrast and focal hierarchy suggest accent strength and climax placement;
- typography personality suggests articulation, rhythmic grouping, repetition, and scale of musical gestures;
- implied motion and energy suggest BPM range, subdivision, groove, build, breakdown, and cadence.

Do not translate one color or shape into one fixed genre. Infer from the complete evidence set and explain why the selected instrumentation, rhythm, and production texture belong to this reference. Reject music that would remain equally plausible after replacing the reference with an unrelated design direction.

## 6. Global consistency tests

- every art, motion, typography, rhythm, camera, transition, BGM, and sound decision is traceable to the approved reference cluster;
- typography looks designed inside the same world as the composition and material treatment;
- typography motion uses the same weight, elasticity, precision, density, and timing family as the rest of the motion system;
- BGM genre, instrumentation, timbre, BPM, rhythmic complexity, phrase spacing, and dynamics match the reference's era, material, density, geometry, and energy;
- edit rhythm and visual choreography fit both the reference and the derived BGM;
- no independent domain introduces a conflicting era, material, energy level, or design culture;
- replacing the approved reference with an unrelated reference would make the complete plan feel wrong.

If the last test fails, the direction is too generic. Rebuild the coherence matrix before writing the prompt.
