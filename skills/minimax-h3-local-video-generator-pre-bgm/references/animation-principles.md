# Twelve Principles of Animation

Apply Disney's Twelve Principles across the complete motion system. Adapt their intensity to the reference's medium, geometry, material, density, and tone. Do not force cartoon deformation onto rigid, minimal, luxury, diagrammatic, or industrial styles.

## The principles

1. **Squash and stretch**
   Preserve volume and identity while communicating force, weight, or elasticity. Use on characters, shapes, panels, and type only when the implied material can deform. For rigid materials, shift the response into camera, shadow, spacing, or attached elements.

2. **Anticipation**
   Prepare important actions with a readable counter-movement, compression, pause, gaze, layout offset, light cue, or typographic pre-shift. Scale anticipation to the size of the following action.

3. **Staging**
   Make the primary idea unmistakable through silhouette, contrast, framing, hierarchy, occlusion, negative space, and controlled secondary motion. Do not let dense graphics compete with the hero action or copy.

4. **Straight-ahead action and pose-to-pose**
   Use pose-to-pose planning for identity, anatomy, typography locks, products, and precise graphic layouts. Use straight-ahead development for particles, fabric, ink, liquid, trails, and organic secondary motion. Combine them when appropriate.

5. **Follow-through and overlapping action**
   Let hair, clothing, shadows, borders, labels, trails, secondary panels, and camera settle after the primary action. Offset their timing rather than stopping every layer simultaneously.

6. **Slow in and slow out**
   Define acceleration and deceleration around key poses or layout locks. Use hard linear or snap motion only when the reference's mechanical, digital, or impact behavior justifies it.

7. **Arcs**
   Favor coherent curved paths for organic bodies, hands, fabric, orbiting motifs, and elastic typography. Use straight paths for measured grids, scans, rails, or mechanical assemblies when the style requires them.

8. **Secondary action**
   Add subordinate motion that reinforces the main action: expression, garment response, sparkle, shadow shift, line trace, label relay, or background module change. Never let it steal focus or break readability.

9. **Timing**
   Use duration, spacing, holds, and rhythm to communicate weight, personality, scale, material, and emphasis. Follow the approved non-uniform rhythm map or video-derived motion signature.

10. **Exaggeration**
    Amplify the clearest pose, silhouette, scale contrast, anticipation, overshoot, typographic crop, or transition impact without breaking identity, anatomy, product geometry, or the reference's tone.

11. **Solid drawing**
    Preserve volume, balance, perspective, anatomy, silhouette, spatial relationships, and consistent construction across poses. For flat motion graphics, maintain coherent shape logic, depth order, alignment, and edge treatment.

12. **Appeal**
    Maintain a clear focal idea, readable personality, attractive silhouette, coherent hierarchy, and memorable final composition. Appeal may be elegant, strange, cute, severe, graphic, or mechanical; it does not mean universally cute.

## Operational use

For every major beat, describe an observable chain:

`staging → anticipation → primary action → graphic or environmental response → follow-through or overlap → moving settle → outgoing momentum carrier → cut or transformation → incoming momentum carrier`

Use pose islands to protect identity and clarity, but keep them alive through reduced-amplitude secondary motion, camera drift, parallax, particles, light travel, fabric or hair overlap, or typographic micro-adjustment. Select one to three primary movers per beat and keep supporting layers subordinate. The overall film should demonstrate all twelve principles where relevant; every object and shot does not need an equally strong display of every principle.

Treat cuts as part of follow-through. The outgoing shot should still contain directional or rotational energy at the cut, and the incoming shot should inherit, redirect, transform, or absorb that energy through camera movement or an element motion. Avoid ending one shot at rest and starting the next at rest. A reversal needs a visible pivot, impact, anticipation, or counter-movement.

Translate the principles into the approved style:

- **Minimal or luxury**: subtle anticipation, precise easing, restrained overlap, one decisive exaggeration, long confident holds.
- **Industrial or mechanical**: rigid volume, measured preparation, rail or scan paths, hard locks, secondary gauges or highlights, minimal deformation.
- **Dense collage or editorial**: clear staging, pose-to-pose panel locks, staggered overlaps, cropped exaggeration, supporting cards that settle after the main carrier.
- **Organic, elastic, or character-led**: visible squash and stretch, curved arcs, stronger anticipation, overlapping hair or fabric, expressive pose changes.
- **Typography-led**: pre-shift or compression, grouped assembly, style-appropriate deformation or reflow, overshoot, foreground or background interaction, lock, and motivated exit.

## Acceptance tests

- the main action is readable before secondary motion is evaluated;
- important actions have proportionate anticipation;
- acceleration, deceleration, weight, and settling are observable;
- organic motion follows arcs and rigid motion follows justified measured paths;
- primary and secondary layers do not stop simultaneously without reason;
- adjacent shots have an explicit outgoing and incoming momentum relationship;
- breath beats, pose islands, and readable locks reduce amplitude without becoming visibly frozen;
- acceleration zones may add short shots or cut bursts instead of reducing the overall number of shot changes;
- exaggeration supports the style and does not break identity or geometry;
- pose-to-pose anchors preserve anatomy, volume, layout, and spelling;
- the final composition has clear appeal and a stable readable layout with subtle style-appropriate living motion.
